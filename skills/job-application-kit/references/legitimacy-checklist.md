# Legitimacy check

Goal: give evidence and a risk rating (low, medium or high). You can find red flags; you cannot prove a job is safe. Never say "safe". State your confidence and what you could not check.

## Red flags in the posting or the process

- Asks the candidate to **pay** for anything (training, equipment, a background check, a "deposit").
- Asks for ID, bank details or a social security number early, or before a written offer.
- Interview only by text or chat app (Telegram, WhatsApp), no video or call with a named person.
- Recruiter writes from a free email domain (Gmail, Outlook) while claiming to represent a company.
- Pay far above market for the role, or vague "unlimited earnings".
- Urgency and pressure ("reply within an hour").
- Very vague duties or company details; generic copy-pasted description.
- Sends a cheque or asks to move money, buy gift cards or receive packages.
- Offer made with no real interview.
- Poor grammar combined with other flags, or a listing that differs from the company's own.

## Verify outside the listing (cite each source)

1. Does the **company's official careers page** list the same role? Search the company name plus "careers".
2. Does the company have a real web presence: a site, a LinkedIn company page with real employees, news coverage? How old is the domain, if you can check?
3. Does the recruiter exist: is the person on the company's team page or LinkedIn, and does their email domain match the company?
4. Search the company name together with "scam", "reviews", "complaints" and read what independent sources say. Note that a lack of results is not proof either way.
5. Does the pay fit market data for this role? (See `pay-check.md`.)
6. For roles offering visa sponsorship, apply the checks in `eligibility-rules.md`.

## Rating

- **Low risk:** official careers page matches, real people and presence, no red flags.
- **Medium:** some items cannot be verified, or one soft flag.
- **High:** any payment request, early ID request, chat-only interview, or listing absent from the official site with other flags.

## Output

A short list: each check, what you found, source link, and the rating with confidence. If medium or high, recommend what the user should verify themselves (for example a video call with a named person) before sharing any personal information.

Reputable general guides exist, for example We Work Remotely's scam checklist. Use current guidance and cite what you use.
